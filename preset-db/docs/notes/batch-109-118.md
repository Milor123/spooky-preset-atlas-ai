## p117

### `Reduce Amplitude < [x] kHz` — the p116 sentence completed, and a Contact safety note
*"…because low frequencies can **sting and tingle uncomfortably**, this control allows you to **lower the voltage for all frequencies lower than the kHz value you specify — 10 KHz is the default.** However, the threshold at which discomfort sets in is different for everyone. **Experiment to find your own threshold by lowering the value about 1 kHz at a time until you find it's 'biting.'**"* (p117)
→ **This is a Contact-Mode comfort/safety instruction, closely quotable.** Low frequencies sting/tingle; 10 kHz default; per-individual threshold; step ~1 kHz at a time.
→ It also means **a preset that leaves this at 10 kHz delivers attenuated amplitude below 10 kHz** — a real, corpus-checkable effect on any preset containing sub-10 kHz frequencies. Directly comparable with p177's duty-cycle rule as a "presets that quietly alter delivery" flag.

### 11. Startup Options (p117) — a three-way distinction on **how a program restarts**
*"Choose whether to start programs automatically on launch. **`Manual Start` leaves control in your hands. `Auto Start` starts all programs anew. `Auto Resume` starts all programs from where you halted.**"* (p117, verbatim)

→ ⚠️ **`Auto Start` and `Auto Resume` are functionally different and the difference matters:** *anew* (from frequency 1 / time 0) vs *from where you halted*. A tool that models a "resume" must not treat it as a restart. This is the cleanest statement in the whole guide of the distinction behind KNOWLEDGE §9d's note that `Hold` is distinct from `Pause`.
→ Screenshot confirms `Manual Start` selected.

### 12. Frequency Limits (Hz) — **this pane is the source of the 76,000–880,000 figure**
- *"**Dr. Rife and Dr. Clark found that frequencies that killed pathogens lay between 76,000 Hz and 880,000 Hz. So 76,000-880,000 Hz is their mortal bandwidth.**"* (p117, verbatim)
  → ⚠️ **This is where `76,000–880,000 Hz` comes from.** KNOWLEDGE §6 currently cites the same span to the p188–189 Hulda Clark Dual Weight Sweep; **p117 is the general, named-source statement** ("mortal bandwidth") and p188 is just a preset that happens to use it. Prefer p117 as the citation for the span.
- *"When programs were being developed for relatively affordable Rife machines, these units were unable to transmit frequencies that high — **most had a top limit of 100,000 Hz.**"* (p117)
  → **100 kHz is the historical cap stated here** (KNOWLEDGE §6 records "10 kHz/100 kHz" from elsewhere; p117 sources the 100 kHz figure).
- **Why the pane exists (p117, verbatim) — the most useful sentence in this range:**
  *"As a result, **the MORs had to be transposed down so they could be used — making them less effective.** Spooky² can transmit up to 25,000,000 Hz, so there's no longer any need to be bound by the old restrictions — **we can simply transpose the low sub-harmonics back up so that they become more effective.** You can do that by **defining your desired bandwidth — upper and lower frequency limits — in this pane.**"*
  → **The guide's own justification for transposition** — historical down-transposition made old MORs less effective, and defining a modern bandwidth lets you transpose them back **up**. This is the software-level counterpart to p107/p149's "transposition is killing-only" (`Frequency Multiplier`). **Two distinct transposition mechanisms exist and must not be conflated:** (a) `Frequency Multiplier`, killing-only; (b) Frequency Limits + Harmonic Menus, applied to Out 2 for carrier/harmonic purposes. p118's `Out 1/Out 2` line confirms (b) targets Out 2.
- **`> [x]`** — *"enter the upper delimiter in Hz here for Out 1 and Out 2. Spooky² will not create a frequency higher than this."*
- **`< [x]`** — *"enter the lower delimiter in Hz here for Out1 and Out 2. Spooky² will not create a frequency lower than this."*
  → **These are hard output clamps**, applied per Out, and they operate by *dropping* frequencies outside the band, not by failing.
- **`Use Harmonic Menus`** — *"choose the harmonic system you wish to use for the transposition for Out 1 and Out 2. **Spooky² will create the lowest harmonic inside the bandwidth.**"*
  → **This last clause is the algorithm, and p118's worked example confirms it exactly (see p118 below).**

### ★ The `Botulinum VEGA` worked example (p118) — **the single most computable passage in this batch**
*"**Example: The `Botulinum VEGA` program has one low frequency — 518 Hz. Enter 880000 in the `>` field, and 76000 in the `<` field (the pathogenic bandwidth), then tick `Out 2`.**"*
*"**Choosing 8x transmits 518 and 265216. Octave creates 518 and 132608. Decade yields 518 and 518000. Fibonacci gives 518 and 120694. Experiment – and use what works best.**"*

**INFERRED algorithm, verified arithmetically against all four published results** (I checked each):

| System | Guide's result | Multiplier (result ÷ 518) | Why that multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| `8x` | 265,216 | **512** = 8³ | 518 × 8² = 33,152 (below 76,000); × 8³ = 265,216 → **inside band** |
| `Octave` | 132,608 | **256** = 2⁸ | 518 × 2⁷ = 66,304 (below 76,000); × 2⁸ = 132,608 → **inside band** |
| `Decade` | 518,000 | **1,000** = 10³ | 518 × 10² = 51,800 (below); × 10³ = 518,000 → **inside band** |
| `Fibonacci` | 120,694 | **233** | 233 is a genuine Fibonacci number; 518 × 233 = 120,694 → **inside band** |

→ **The rule, stated by p117 and confirmed by all four of p118's numbers: for the chosen harmonic system, multiply the program frequency by the *lowest* member of that system that lands the product inside the `[lower limit, upper limit]` band; transmit the original on one Out and that product on the other.**
→ **This resolves KNOWLEDGE §12.14's dangling `76,000–880,000` and gives a fully deterministic, re-implementable rule.** It also retro-explains the p188 Dual Weight Sweep recipe.
→ **Corollary for a tool:** a frequency limit band is not merely a filter — it *actively generates a second, higher frequency*. A preset with non-zero `>` or `<` limits and a non-`Octave` harmonic menu is emitting frequencies that **are not in the program's frequency list.** A frequency-count or "what does this preset transmit" report built only from the program list will be **wrong** for any such preset.
→ The guide's own caveat on the worked example: *"Experiment – and use what works best."*

### 13. F2 Control (p118) — **the mechanism behind "Contact Mode needs a dynamic carrier"**
- *"yet another unique Spooky² technology which allows you to **build a second frequency into your waveform**. Its advantage is that you can use it to **automatically add a high frequency dynamic carrier to each frequency in your program.**"* (p118, verbatim)
  → ⭐ **This is the documented mechanism for KNOWLEDGE §7 rule 17.** A dynamic carrier is not something you hand-add per frequency; `F2 Control` applies it *automatically to every frequency in the program*. That materially changes how a Contact-mode preset should be validated.
- **`F2 = F1 X [x] Hz`** (field label as printed, garbled — it is a multiplier): *"enter your **harmonic multiplier** here. **We recommend using octave or decade multipliers**, although **integers like 3, 7, 11, etc., have had interesting results too.**"*
  → **3, 7, 11 named explicitly** — and **11 is the Holland 11th Harmonic** (F2 = 11 × F1, KNOWLEDGE §4/§9d). This is the first place in this range where the `Harmonic/Holland` item from the known list is named, and it is named *here*, as a plain integer multiplier rather than under a "Holland" label.
- **`[x] degrees`** — *"this allows you to specify a **phase angle**. This is a very technical subject, of interest mainly to engineers and experienced Rife users."*
  → **The only definition of the phase-angle field in the guide.** It corresponds to the `Phase 0 / 0 °` field in Signal Settings (p109) and the trailing `0 0` in the F2 Control row (p118). **LEAD, NOT A CONFIRMATION:** KNOWLEDGE §12.14 lists `VI Angle` as undefined; nothing on p118 uses the string "VI Angle", so that gap is **still open** — do not close it on this evidence.
- **`Checkbox`** — *"ticking this activates the F2 Control."* → the unchecked box above the `Add F1 to F2` dropdown is the F2 master switch.
- **The dropdown reads `Add F1 to F2`** (p118 screenshot, unchecked). → This is the **same control name p146 uses for the Holland 11th Harmonic additive mechanism** (KNOWLEDGE §9d). Two pages, one control. Sourced link, not inference.
- **Discrepancy between screenshots:** p109's F2 row shows multiplier **8**; p118's shows **1**. Two different presets, not a changing default.
